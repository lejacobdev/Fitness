
# Student Athlete Companion — Complete Build Plan

2026-09-22 · owner @owner · one prompt, one continuous build, no placeholders

**Read this first.** This is a finished specification, meant to be pasted as ONE prompt to a fresh Claude Code session and built straight through to a submittable app — not a prototype, not a v0.1, nothing marked "coming soon".

**Working agreement**

- Build in the milestone order of §22. Each has acceptance criteria; do not start the next before the current one passes.
- **Nothing is generative and nothing is borrowed.** No AI or LLM API of any kind. No licensed, scraped or imported dataset. No stock or commissioned art. No third-party Swift packages. Every sport profile, drill, illustration and sentence of coaching is authored or generated by code that ships in this repository.
- **No placeholders.** No "coming soon", no flag gating an unfinished feature, no screen that exists but does not work. If it is in this plan, it ships working.
- **The app never predicts injury and never diagnoses anything.** See §2 and §11 — this is a safety rule and an App Review rule, not a stylistic one.
- **It works with the phone in aeroplane mode.** A rink, a field and a weight room have no signal. Every feature except the subscription purchase itself must work fully offline, from first launch.
- Commit and push after every working change.
- Nothing to wait on: §23 records decisions already made, not questions.

**What "from scratch" means here.** Swift, SwiftUI, SwiftData, CloudKit, HealthKit and StoreKit are the language and the operating system — you cannot write an iOS app without them any more than you can write a sentence without an alphabet. They are not "dependencies" in the sense this plan rules out. What is ruled out is _content and data owned by someone else_: no AI service, no exercise or drill database, no sports dataset, no stock imagery, no analytics SDK, no third-party package of any kind in the Xcode project.


## 1. What we are building

**The promise, in one sentence:** you tell it what sport you play, and it becomes your daily training companion — asking how you slept, knowing when your next game is, and telling you exactly what to do today to get better at the specific thing you want to get better at.

This is not a gym logger that happens to know about sports. The organising fact is **the season**: a student athlete's training should look completely different in August than in November, and completely different the day before a game than three days after one. Almost nothing on the App Store gets that right for a 16-year-old.

**The daily loop**

- Morning: _How did you sleep?_ Four taps, under fifteen seconds.
- The app shows today's session, already adjusted for how the athlete answered and how close the next game is.
- They train, ticking off sets and drills — from the phone, or from the Watch when the phone is in a locker.
- Afterwards: one question, _how hard was that?_ That single number drives the load tracking in §11.
- Weekly: a plain-language review of what improved, what slipped, and what to do about it.

**The two things that make it worth opening every day**

- **It knows your calendar.** "Game Friday" changes Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday automatically (§10).
- **It answers the actual question athletes ask.** Not "give me a leg workout" but "my shot is weak, I play soccer, I have a game in a week" — answered through a menu, with real drills and real exercises, in §8.

**Explicitly out of scope, permanently** — not deferred, simply not this product:

- No social feed, teammates, leaderboards or sharing. It doubles the moderation surface, and for minors it multiplies the risk.
- No coach or parent dashboards in v1. Multi-user accounts change the privacy model completely (§2).
- No video form analysis. Pose-grading a squat from a phone camera is its own product and a safety liability.
- No injury diagnosis, rehab protocols or return-to-play decisions. Those belong to an athletic trainer or a doctor.
- No recruiting, scouting, stat tracking or game film.
- No Android, no web. The Watch companion is the second platform.


## 2. Your users are minors — read before designing anything

Most of this app's users will be **13 to 18 years old**. That single fact constrains more of the design than the sport selection does, and it is the part a naive build gets wrong. Everything in this section is a requirement, not a recommendation.

**Age gate at first launch, before anything else.** Ask date of birth (a date picker, not a yes/no — a yes/no gate is trivially lied past and Apple knows it). Branch:

- **Under 13:** do not create an account. Explain plainly that the app is for 13 and over and stop. Supporting under-13s means COPPA verifiable parental consent, the Kids Category, and a materially different app; it is out of scope and §23 records that decision.
- **13–17:** full app, with the minor-specific rules below applied and a one-screen explanation, written for a teenager, of exactly what the app stores and that it never leaves their phone.
- **18+:** same app, same rules. The protections below are good defaults for everyone, so nothing branches on age after this point except the fuelling rules in §13.

**Hard rules, enforced in code**

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Zero analytics, zero tracking, zero advertising identifiers, no third-party SDK of any kind, ever | Required for minors, and nothing in this app needs them |
| **Sign in with Apple is the only way in.** No passwords, no email collection, Private Relay respected, no other provider | Minimum possible data collected, and it satisfies Apple's 4.8 login requirement outright |
| Wellness and training data lives on our own backend and nowhere else — never a third party, never an advertiser, never an AI service | §3. It syncs so a new phone restores everything; §20 covers exactly what storing minors' health data obligates us to do |
| No body-composition tracking, no body-fat estimates, no progress photos, no weight goals | Body-image features aimed at teenage athletes are how you cause harm. Body weight is optional, and never commented on |
| No calorie deficits, ever, for any user under 18 | §13. Under-fuelling a growing athlete is the most common real harm in youth sport nutrition |
| Never predict injury, never diagnose, never advise on return to play | §11, and App Review guideline 1.4.1 |
| Always a companion to a coach, never a replacement | The NSCA's position on youth resistance training is explicit that qualified supervision is what makes youth training safe |

**Programming has to respect who is lifting.** The NSCA's guidance for children and adolescents is **2–3 nonconsecutive days a week, 1–3 sets of 6–15 repetitions**, technique before load, across the major muscle groups. The generator in §10 clamps to that envelope for anyone under 18 and for anyone who tells the app they are new to lifting, regardless of what their sport profile would otherwise suggest. **No one-rep-max testing and no percentage-of-1RM prescriptions for under-18s** — the app prescribes rep ranges and effort, never a max.

**A standing disclaimer, visible not buried.** On the onboarding screen, in Settings, and at the foot of every generated plan: _general training information, not medical advice; talk to your coach or athletic trainer before changing how you train; stop and tell an adult if something hurts._ Any free-text note mentioning pain, a head knock, dizziness, chest symptoms or numbness triggers a fixed, non-negotiable response pointing at their athletic trainer, and the app will not generate a session for that body area until they clear it themselves.


## 3. Architecture: there is no server

**Offline-first client, thin backend.** The athlete signs in with Apple, so a new phone restores their whole history; the sport catalogue is downloaded in per-sport packs rather than shipped whole; and everything they do day to day works with no signal, because a rink, a field and a weight room usually have none.

**The backend** — Node 20 / Express / Prisma / PostgreSQL in Docker Compose, the same stack and CI shape as the owner's existing app, because that shape is already proven end to end. It does four things and nothing else:

- **Accounts.** Sign in with Apple only. Verify the identity token server-side against Apple's published keys — pin RS256, check issuer, audience, expiry and nonce. Store the `sub` and nothing else; no email, no name, no Apple data beyond that stable identifier.
- **Sync.** Plans, sessions, logged sets, check-ins and skill blocks. Last-write-wins per record with a client-supplied `clientId` for idempotency, so a flaky reconnect can never duplicate a logged set.
- **Content packs.** Per-sport bundles, versioned and served over HTTPS with an `ETag`. One small `core` pack (the general exercise library, the rig, the shared drills) plus one pack per sport.
- **Entitlement.** Subscription state, written only by the App Store Server Notifications webhook (§18). The client never tells the server it is Pro.

**Sizes, measured not guessed.** Because §9's illustrations are joint-angle data rather than images, the packs are small: the core pack is a couple of megabytes, a single sport pack is well under one. The whole catalogue for all 70+ sports is single-digit megabytes. **So packs are not solving a storage problem** — they exist so content can be updated without an App Store release, and so §4 has something real to gate. Build them anyway; just do not tell users it is about saving space, because it is not, and a curious teenager will notice.

**What works with no signal, for everyone including free users**

| Works offline always | Needs connectivity |
|---|---|
| Today's session, this week's plan, the daily check-in | Signing in the first time |
| Logging sets, drills, RPE — queued and drained on reconnect | Downloading a sport pack you do not have yet |
| Every downloaded sport's full library, drills, illustrations, skill menus | Browsing or switching to a sport whose pack is not downloaded |
| The readiness engine, the coach engine, plan generation — all of it runs on device | Restoring onto a brand-new device |
| Everything already logged, and all history held locally | Buying or restoring a subscription |

**A free user always has at least one sport downloaded and fully usable offline.** Gating a 15-year-old out of logging their own session at a rink with no bars would lose the user permanently; §4 gates _how many_ sports and _what depth_, never their own data.

**Writes are queued, never lost.** Every mutation is written to the local store first and enqueued; the drain is idempotent on `clientId`. The UI never blocks on the network and never shows a spinner for something already saved locally. An athlete who is offline for a fortnight loses nothing.

**Storing minors' health data is a real obligation, taken on deliberately.** CloudKit's private database would have kept it off our servers entirely, but it cannot serve content packs, cannot survive a move off Apple platforms, and gives no way to fix bad data. The trade is accepted here — in exchange, §20 requires encryption at rest, a hard-delete path that actually deletes, no retention of anything not needed to render history, and no backup of wellness data to anywhere but our own database.


## 4. Free vs Pro

The free tier has to be genuinely useful to an athlete who plays one sport — that is most high schoolers — and Pro has to be obviously worth it to the multi-sport athlete and the one chasing a specific goal.

| Capability | Free | Pro |
|---|---|---|
| Sports downloaded for offline use | **1** | Unlimited |
| Browsing other sports' libraries | On Wi-Fi or cellular | Anywhere, downloaded |
| Daily check-in and readiness (§11) | Yes | Yes |
| Today's session and the weekly plan | Yes | Yes |
| Logging sets, drills and RPE, online or off | Yes | Yes |
| Apple Watch app | Yes | Yes |
| Named skill menu (§8) | 3 skill blocks per month | Unlimited |
| Game-day periodisation (§10) | Next game only | Full season calendar, multi-game weeks, tournaments |
| History and trends | Last 30 days | Everything, with per-quality progression |
| Weekly coach report (§12) | Weekly | After every session, plus on demand |
| Position-specific profiles | Sport default | Every position, switchable |
| Fuelling guidance (§13) | Yes | Yes |
| Data export | No | Yes |

The check-in, the plan, logging and the Watch are free forever and work offline. They are what makes the app a daily habit, and a daily habit is what makes Pro worth buying.

**Paywall rules App Review enforces** — each of these has caused real rejections:

- Digital features must use in-app purchase (3.1.1). No external payment links.
- The paywall must show subscription length, price per period, what is included, and **links to the Terms of Use (EULA) and Privacy Policy**. Missing in-app links is the single most common subscription rejection.
- A working **Restore Purchases** button is mandatory.
- Account deletion must never sit behind the paywall.
- No "free trial" wording unless an introductory offer is actually configured in App Store Connect.
- The reviewer **cannot complete a purchase** — see §18 for the demo account that must exist with Pro already granted.

**Selling to minors, which is the part most plans miss.** A 15-year-old cannot enter a contract, and most will be buying through Family Sharing with Ask to Buy. So: no countdown timers, no "only today" urgency, no dark patterns, and no interstitial paywall thrown up mid-session. State the price plainly in the local currency, mention that a parent may need to approve the purchase, and make the free tier's limits visible up front rather than discovered after a month. Apple reviews apps used by minors with this specifically in mind, and it is also simply the right way to treat them.


## 5. The sport catalogue

The NFHS recorded **70 different sports** plus 15 Unified and 16 Adapted sports across its member associations in 2025–26, with 8.4 million participants. The NCAA sponsors championships in roughly 25 sports and added four more at its 2026 Convention — acrobatics and tumbling, stunt, Division II bowling and Division III women's wrestling — while flag football entered the Emerging Sports for Women programme.

**Ship all of them.** This is affordable because a sport is a **profile**, not a library — see §6. Each sport is one record of a few dozen numbers and tags, and the drills and exercises come from one shared library that every sport draws on.

**What a ****`Sport`**** record holds**

| Field | Example (ice hockey) |
|---|---|
| `slug`, `name` | `ice-hockey`, "Ice Hockey" |
| `governing` | `[NFHS, NCAA]` — drives whether it appears under "high school" or "college" filters |
| `season` | `WINTER` (plus the typical month range, so §10 knows where in the season today falls) |
| `qualityProfile` | Weighted demand across the §6 qualities — for hockey: acceleration 0.9, change of direction 0.9, rotational power 0.8, single-leg stability 0.8, anaerobic capacity 0.9, aerobic base 0.5, grip 0.4, overhead stability 0.2 |
| `positions[]` | Goaltender, Defence, Forward — each with its **own** quality profile overriding the sport's. A goaltender's hip mobility and lateral power demands are nothing like a winger's |
| `skills[]` | The menu in §8: shooting power, shooting accuracy, skating speed, edge work, puck handling, checking strength |
| `commonLoadAreas[]` | Hips/groin, lower back — used **only** to bias prehab and mobility work into plans. Never to warn about or predict injury (§11) |
| `contactLevel` | `COLLISION` — affects recovery spacing after games in §10 |
| `typicalSessionLength`, `typicalWeeklyGames` | Sets realistic defaults so the first generated plan is not absurd |

**Coverage tiers, all shipped, differing only in depth of the skill menu.** Every one of the 70+ sports gets a full profile, positions, and a complete training plan from day one. The **named skill menu** in §8 is authored deepest for the ~25 sports that cover the overwhelming majority of participation (football, basketball, track and field, baseball, softball, soccer, volleyball, cross country, wrestling, tennis, golf, swimming and diving, ice hockey, field hockey, lacrosse, competitive spirit, flag football, bowling, gymnastics, water polo, rowing, skiing, rifle, fencing, ultimate) — 8–14 named skills each. The long tail gets 4–6 skills covering its dominant actions. **No sport ships without a working skill menu**; the tiering is about how granular the menu is, never whether it exists.

**Multi-sport athletes are the norm, not an edge case.** A high schooler who plays soccer in autumn and runs track in spring is extremely common. The athlete selects any number of sports with a date range for each; §10 blends the profiles by whichever season is currently active and handles the overlap week when one ends and another begins.


## 6. The athletic-quality model

This is the spine that makes 70 sports tractable, and it is the single most important idea in the plan. **Sports do not need their own exercise libraries, because sports do not have unique physical demands — they have unique ****_mixtures_**** of shared demands.** A hockey player's crossover and a soccer player's cut are both lateral force production off one leg.

So: one library, tagged by quality; each sport a weighting across those qualities.

**The qualities** (~18, fixed, versioned):

| Group | Qualities |
|---|---|
| Speed | Acceleration (0–10m), maximum velocity, change of direction, lateral power, deceleration / eccentric braking |
| Power | Vertical power, horizontal power, rotational power, overhead / throwing power, reactive strength (stiffness) |
| Strength | Lower-body max strength, upper-body push, upper-body pull, trunk / anti-rotation, grip |
| Endurance | Aerobic base, anaerobic capacity, repeat-sprint ability |
| Control | Single-leg stability, hip mobility, shoulder / overhead stability, ankle stiffness, balance and landing mechanics |

**Every exercise and drill in §7 is tagged** with the qualities it develops and a weight for each (1.0 primary, 0.5 secondary), exactly like the muscle weighting in a strength app but one layer up. Every sport and position is a weighting across the same list. Plan generation is then a matching problem, not an authoring problem: _this athlete plays this position, which demands these qualities, in this proportion; the library contains these items that develop them; here is the session._

**Why this is worth the discipline:**

- Adding the 71st sport is one record, and it immediately has a complete, correct training plan drawn from the whole library.
- Adding one new exercise improves every sport that needs its qualities, at once.
- "Get better at X" in §8 becomes a real query rather than a hand-written answer per sport.
- The weekly review in §12 can say _which quality_ an athlete has been neglecting, which is far more useful to a 16-year-old than "you skipped leg day".

**Exercises are still tagged with muscles too** (the same ~45-muscle model any strength app needs) — qualities drive selection, muscles drive the red highlighting in §9 and the recovery spacing in §10. Both, not either.


## 7. The exercise and drill library

**Nothing is imported.** No exercise database, no drill library, no scraped content, no licensed video. Everything is authored in this repository and expanded by code, as in §6.

**Two kinds of item, one table.**

- **Exercises** — general physical preparation. Trap-bar deadlift, split squat, med-ball rotational throw, Nordic curl, pogo hop, band pull-apart. Sport-agnostic; selected by quality.
- **Drills** — sport-specific technical or tactical work, requiring that sport's equipment and surface. Soccer instep-drive progression, hockey edge-work figure eights, volleyball approach-and-swing footwork, sprint block starts.

**Authoring plan: ~200 base exercises + ~400 drills, expanded to well over 1,500 items.** The base items are hand-authored with real setup, execution, coaching cues and common mistakes written from movement principles. Expansion then applies parameter rules once, not per item: equipment substitution (barbell → dumbbell → band → bodyweight, with the quality weights adjusted by rule), unilateral/bilateral, tempo emphasis (eccentric, isometric, explosive), and for drills, constraint variations (opposed/unopposed, static/moving, one touch/two touch). An athlete with nothing but a field and a ball still receives a complete, correct plan — the equipment filter never produces an empty session, because every quality has a bodyweight or minimal-equipment path by construction, and a test asserts exactly that.

**Every item carries:**

`slug` (permanent) · `name` · `kind` (exercise or drill) · `qualities[]` with weights · `muscles[]` with weights · `equipment[]` · `surface` (field, court, ice, pool, gym, anywhere) · `minAge` and `supervisionLevel` · `setup[]` · `execution[]` · `cues[]` · `mistakes[]` · `progressions[]` and `regressions[]` · `substitutes[]` · `defaultDose` (sets × reps, or distance, or time, or contacts) · `restSeconds` · `startPose`/`endPose` for §9.

**Youth gating is a field, not an afterthought.** Anything with meaningful technical risk or spinal loading (barbell back squat, power clean, depth jump from height, heavy overhead press) carries `minAge` and `supervisionLevel: COACHED`. The generator in §10 excludes `COACHED` items unless the athlete has confirmed in Settings that they train under a coach's supervision, and never prescribes them to under-14s at all. Regressions always exist, so excluding them never leaves a hole.

**Plyometric volume is capped by rule.** Youth plyometric work is prescribed in **ground contacts**, not "sets", with a weekly ceiling that scales with age and training history, spaced at least 48 hours apart. This is the most commonly over-prescribed category in youth training and the one where the generator must be conservative by default.

**A completeness test blocks the build**: every item has at least one quality with weight ≥ 0.5, non-empty cues and execution, a resolvable substitute list, a valid dose, and a pose pair. Every quality has at least one bodyweight-only item. Every sport's profile resolves to at least 40 eligible items at every equipment level.


## 8. "I want to get better at shooting" — the skill menu

This is the feature the athlete will actually tell their friends about, and **it is a menu, not a chat box.** Three taps, no typing, no model:

> **Sport** → Soccer · **I want to improve** → Shooting power · **My next game is** → in 7 days

and the app returns a dated plan of exercises and drills that fits between now and the game.

**How it works — a three-link chain, all of it data.**

- **Skill → qualities.** Each named skill in a sport's `skills[]` carries a weighted list of the physical qualities that actually underpin it. This mapping is the authored intelligence of the app, and it is written once per skill.
- **Qualities → items.** Query the §7 library for the highest-weighted matches, filtered by the athlete's equipment, surface, age and supervision.
- **Items → a dated block.** Hand the selection to the §10 generator with the game date as the anchor, so the heavy work lands early and the last 48 hours are sharp rather than exhausting.

**Worked example: "Soccer · Shooting power · game in 7 days."** The mapping is not guesswork — it follows the biomechanics. Shot velocity is produced by planting hard and braking the support leg fast, then swinging the kicking leg through; research on shot velocity in elite players associates faster shots with eccentric braking ability, and accuracy with maximal strength relative to body mass and ankle reactive strength, while plyometric and explosive training has been shown to improve maximal instep kick velocity by roughly 12%.

So `soccer.shooting-power` maps to: eccentric braking / deceleration **1.0**, rotational power **0.9**, horizontal and vertical power **0.8**, lower-body max strength relative to bodyweight **0.7**, ankle stiffness and reactive strength **0.7**, hip mobility **0.5**, single-leg stability **0.5**.

Which resolves to a week like:

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| −7 (today) | Strength emphasis: trap-bar deadlift or goblet squat by equipment, Bulgarian split squat, Nordic or slider hamstring curl, med-ball rotational throw × low reps, plus the instep-drive technical progression unopposed |
| −6 | Drill day: plant-and-strike progression, one-touch finishing off a rolling ball, weak-foot volume |
| −5 | Plyometrics within the contact cap: depth drop to stick, bounding, pogo series. Plus mobility for hips and ankles |
| −4 | Light technical, full recovery emphasis |
| −3 | Complex pairing: a heavy set followed by an explosive one, then strike volume while fresh |
| −2 | Sharpening only: low-volume strikes at full intent, no lifting |
| −1 | Primer: short activation, a handful of maximal-intent strikes, early night. Explicitly _not_ a hard session |
| Game day | Warm-up sequence only |

Every cell is generated by the same rules for every sport and skill — nothing above is hand-written for soccer beyond the skill→quality weights and the drill entries themselves.

**The screen also explains itself.** Each item shows _why it is there_ ("builds the eccentric braking your plant leg does when you strike"), written by the engine from the skill→quality mapping, so the athlete learns the reasoning rather than following a black box. That sentence is assembled from the data, not generated by a model.

**Realistic expectations, stated in the UI.** Seven days of work will not transform a shot. The plan screen says what is actually achievable in the window — for under two weeks, the honest answer is "sharper and better coordinated, not fundamentally stronger" — and offers the 8-week version of the same block for the off-season. Overpromising to a 15-year-old is both dishonest and how the app loses them in month two.


## 9. Illustrations

**One rigged figure. Everything else is data, not art.** No stock imagery, no commissioned illustration, no video, nothing traced. A single 2D figure is drawn once as clean uniform-stroke line art, and every exercise and drill poses and colours that one rig from its own data.

**Job 1 — the muscle map.** Draw the rig's ~90 muscle regions as individual SVG paths, front and back, each id matching a muscle slug, left and right separately. Any item then renders itself: primary muscles solid red (`#E5383B`), secondary at ~45% opacity, stabilisers at ~18%, the rest base tone. A build script emits `MuscleMapPaths.swift` — a `[MuscleSlug: String]` of raw path data — rendered with SwiftUI `Path` and `Canvas`. No SVG library, and it works unchanged on watchOS. Colour is never the only channel: the muscle names are listed beneath and read out by VoiceOver.

**Job 2 — the pose.** The same rig has joints (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle, spine, left and right). A pose is a `[Joint: AngleDegrees]` dictionary; every item has two, start and end. Rather than posing 1,500 items by hand, author **one canonical pose pair per movement pattern** (roughly 25–35 pairs once drills are included — a squat pattern, a hinge, a lunge, a horizontal push, an instep strike, a skating stride, an overhead throw, a sprint start), and let each item inherit its pattern's pair, then apply the same parameter rules §7 already defines: unilateral mirrors and dims one side, equipment swaps the hand position, tempo emphasis is annotation rather than geometry.

**Sport drills get a prop layer, not a new rig.** A ball, a puck, a bar, a net or a hurdle is a small named shape positioned relative to a joint — `ball at right-foot +12cm` — so a soccer strike and a hockey shot reuse the same body with a different prop and a different pattern. Around a dozen props cover every sport in the catalogue.

**"Animation" is a crossfade.** Looping between the start and end pose over about 1.2 seconds reads as movement and costs nothing beyond the two poses each item already has. No video, nothing to re-render, and it re-themes for dark mode by changing constants.

**No item ships without a pose.** The §7 completeness test asserts that every exercise and drill resolves to a valid start and end pose, that every referenced joint exists on the rig, and that every muscle slug in the data has a matching path id in the SVG — in both directions, because a typo there is an invisibly missing muscle rather than a crash.


## 10. Plan generation and periodisation

Entirely deterministic and seeded, so the same inputs always produce the same plan and any generated week can be reproduced exactly from a bug report.

**The season is the organising fact.** Every plan is generated against **where today sits relative to the athlete's season and their next competition**, which is what almost no consumer fitness app does and what makes this one worth using.

| Phase | When | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Off-season | No competitions for 8+ weeks | Highest volume. Build max strength and aerobic base; technique work on weak qualities; this is when real physical change happens |
| Pre-season | 3–8 weeks out | Volume tapers as intensity climbs. Power, speed and sport-specific conditioning take over from general strength |
| In-season | Competitions weekly | **Maintain, do not build.** Two short quality sessions a week, enough to hold the adaptations, never enough to compromise a game |
| Post-season | 2–4 weeks after the last game | Deliberate unloading. Movement, mobility, other activities. The app actively tells them to back off, which no 16-year-old hears often enough |

**Game-day periodisation — the rules that shift the week.** Given the next competition date, working backwards:

- **− 4 days and earlier:** normal loading for the phase.
- **− 3 days:** last heavy session. Full intensity, reduced volume.
- **− 2 days:** quality over quantity — short, sharp, technical. No lifting to failure.
- **− 1 day:** primer only. Brief activation, a few maximal-intent efforts, explicitly not a hard session, with an early-night nudge.
- **Game day:** the warm-up sequence, nothing else.
- **⬆ 1 day after:** recovery emphasis, scaled by the sport's `contactLevel` — a collision sport gets a genuinely easy day, a golf round does not.
- **Two games in a week:** the block between them collapses to primer-and-recover, and the app says so plainly rather than silently deleting the session.

**Session assembly.** Warm-up matched to the day's qualities → speed or power work while fresh (never after fatigue) → strength → conditioning → mobility and prehab biased toward the sport's `commonLoadAreas`. Ordering is not negotiable: plyometrics and sprint work before lifting, always.

**Dose and progression.** Under-18s and beginners are clamped to the §2 envelope — 2–3 nonconsecutive days, 1–3 sets of 6–15 reps, no percentage-of-1RM prescriptions, no max testing. Progression is by reps and quality of movement before load, and plyometric work is counted in ground contacts against a weekly cap. The generator surfaces a plain reason for each item ("this is here because your position needs change of direction, and you have done none this week"), written by the engine from the profile weights.

**Every plan is adjustable and nothing is mandatory.** The athlete can swap any item (§7's substitutes), mark equipment unavailable, or say "I only have 30 minutes" — the generator re-solves within the constraint rather than refusing.


## 11. The daily check-in and readiness engine

**The morning check-in is four taps and under fifteen seconds.** Anything longer will not survive a school morning, and a check-in nobody completes is worse than none.

| Question | Scale |
|---|---|
| How did you sleep? | 1–5, with hours slept pulled from HealthKit when available so they only confirm |
| How sore are you? | 1–5, with an optional body-area tap |
| How is your energy? | 1–5 |
| How is stress / school feeling? | 1–5 |

After every training session, exactly one more question: **how hard was that, 1–10?** Multiplied by session minutes, that gives session-RPE load — the single most useful number in the whole app, and the reason the one question is never skipped or buried.

**Readiness is scored against the athlete's own baseline, never a population norm.** A teenager who always sleeps six hours is not "in the red" every day; what matters is _today versus their normal_. Keep a rolling 28-day mean and standard deviation per item, compute a z-score, and combine into a readiness band — green, amber, or red — only once at least 7 days of history exist. Before that, the app shows the raw answers and says it is still learning their baseline, rather than inventing a score from nothing.

**What readiness actually does.** It adjusts today's session, visibly and reversibly:

- **Green:** the plan as generated.
- **Amber:** volume trimmed ~20%, intensity held, plyometric contacts cut. The app says which and why.
- **Red:** the session becomes movement, mobility and an early night, with one line explaining that this is the training decision, not a punishment. The athlete can override in one tap — it is a suggestion, and treating a teenager's own judgement as irrelevant is how they stop answering honestly.

**Load tracking, and the line this app will not cross.** Track acute load (7-day rolling sRPE) against chronic load (28-day), and show the ratio as a trend with a flag when the week jumps sharply. This is standard practice and genuinely useful for spotting a spike.

**It must never be presented as injury risk.** The acute:chronic workload ratio is widely used for monitoring but does not predict injury, and the "sweet spot" figure everyone reproduces is methodologically flawed — when the data is treated properly the relationship largely disappears. So the app says _"your training load is up 40% on your four-week average — that is a big jump, consider easing the next couple of days"_ and never _"you are at elevated risk of injury."_ The first is true and useful. The second is unsupported, and as a health claim about a minor it is also an App Review problem under 1.4.1. Encode this as a lint rule over the template bank in §12: no template may contain the words injury, risk, damage or safe/unsafe.

**Sleep gets special treatment because it is the one thing that actually moves.** Adolescents need roughly 8–10 hours, and almost none get it. Chart sleep against training load and the athlete's own performance notes, and make that chart the first thing on the trends screen — for this audience it is the highest-leverage behaviour in the entire app.


## 12. The coach engine

**No model, no API, no cost.** A plain Swift function, `computeCoachReport(for:)`, turns the numbers from §10 and §11 into sentences. The engines decide what is true; this decides what to say first and how to word it.

**Four steps**

- **Score every candidate talking point.** Each finding — a neglected quality, a load spike, a sleep trend, a missed week, a personal best, a streak of completed sessions — gets a priority from a fixed severity weight multiplied by a streak multiplier, `1 + 0.3 × min(consecutiveWeeks, 4)`. Something ignored for a month escalates in tone automatically, and it is arithmetic rather than judgement. Good news carries a fixed high score so it is never buried under a warning.
- **Pick.** Top point becomes the headline, the next one or two become observations, skipping any that overlap the same quality so the same fact is never said twice. Up to three recommendations, each pulled from items the §7 library already selected — this engine never chooses an exercise itself.
- **Render from a template pool.** Every `(findingCode, streakTier)` pair maps to a pool of 4–6 hand-written sentences with slots: _"You have trained change of direction once in three weeks — for a {position} that is the quality your game leans on most."_ Selection is seeded on `hash(userId + isoWeek + findingCode)` so it is reproducible and testable, and the index used last week for that finding is excluded so two consecutive weeks never read identically.
- **Compose.** Headline, observations, recommendations, and a closing line drawn from an encouragement pool gated by whether the week was good, mixed, or poor.

**Write for a 15-year-old, not a sports scientist.** "Your legs have done almost nothing explosive this week" beats "lower-body reactive strength volume is below threshold." Keep the jargon behind an optional tap.

**Structurally safe.** Because every sentence is a reviewed template, the coach cannot invent a drill, hallucinate a number, diagnose anything, or produce the forbidden injury-risk language — the lint rule in §11 runs over the whole bank in CI. That is a stronger guarantee than prompt instructions to a model could ever give.


## 13. Fuelling and hydration

**The rule that governs this whole section: a growing athlete's problem is almost never eating too much.** It is eating too little for what they are doing, especially during a season with two-a-days. This app therefore helps them **fuel**, and never helps them cut.

**Hard rules, enforced in code, not just in copy**

- **No calorie deficit is ever generated for a user under 18.** No weight-loss goal, no "cutting" mode, no target weight, no body-fat estimate, no progress photos.
- **No calorie counting as the primary interface.** For this audience, logging every gram is both unnecessary and a known risk pathway. The default is a simple plate-and-timing model, not a numeric ledger.
- **Never comment on body shape, weight or appearance.** Not in the coach engine, not in a template, not anywhere. The §12 lint rule covers this vocabulary too.
- An 18+ user who explicitly asks for a deficit gets a conservative one with floors, and a signpost toward a registered dietitian. Under 18, the feature does not exist.

**What it actually does instead**

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Fuel around sessions | What and roughly when to eat before training, after training, and on game day, sized to session length and time of day |
| Hydration | A simple daily target adjusted for session count and heat, with a reminder pattern rather than a nag |
| Plate guidance | Portion-based, not gram-based: a protein portion, a carbohydrate portion, colour on the plate. The standard, sane approach for teenagers |
| Game-day timing | The one genuinely high-value piece: what to eat 3 hours out, 1 hour out, and between games in a tournament |
| Under-fuelling flags | If an athlete reports very low energy alongside a heavy training week, the coach engine suggests eating more, and mentions talking to an adult — never a diagnosis, never the phrase RED-S or any clinical term |
| Optional weight logging | Off by default. If turned on, it is a plain line on a chart with no goal, no target, no commentary |

**Everything here is general nutrition guidance, written from established sports-nutrition principles and labelled as such** — with the standing line that an athlete with specific dietary needs, a medical condition or a restrictive diet should talk to a doctor or dietitian. No food database, no barcode scanner, no imported nutrition dataset: that whole apparatus belongs to a calorie-counting app, which this deliberately is not.


## 14. Data model

**Two stores that mirror each other.** SwiftData on the device is the source of truth for the UI and works offline; Postgres behind Prisma is the source of truth for the account and the sync. Same field names on both sides so the mapping is mechanical.

**Catalogue (read-only, arrives in packs, never user-edited)** — `Sport`, `Position`, `Skill`, `Quality`, `Muscle`, `Item` (exercise or drill), `ItemQuality`, `ItemMuscle`, `PosePair`, `Prop`, `PackManifest` (slug, version, checksum, size).

**Athlete data (read-write, synced)**

                 displayName, birthDate, unitSystem,
                 trainsUnderCoach: Bool, equipmentAvailable: [String],
                 proUntil, originalTransactionId, createdAt

**Three decisions worth defending.** `clientId` on every writable row is the idempotency key that makes the offline queue safe to retry — without it a flaky reconnect duplicates a whole session. `proUntil` is written **only** by the App Store notification handler in §18, never by the client. And `CheckIn` is unique per athlete per day, so a second check-in edits the first rather than creating a duplicate day that would corrupt every rolling baseline in §11.


## 15. iPhone app

SwiftUI, iOS 17+, portrait, five tabs. Every screen needs its loading, empty, error and **offline** states designed up front — offline is the normal case here, not the exception.

**Onboarding.** Age gate (§2) → Sign in with Apple → pick your sport, then position → season dates (with sensible defaults per sport, editable) → equipment you actually have access to → do you train under a coach → HealthKit permission with the reason stated _before_ the system sheet → downloads your sport pack. Six screens, skippable past the sport choice, and the app is usable the moment the pack lands.

**Tab 1 — Today.** The whole product in one screen. The check-in card if it is not done yet (four taps, inline, no navigation). Then today's session: title, the qualities it targets, estimated minutes, a large Start button. A readiness line if the plan was adjusted, saying what changed and why, with a one-tap override. A countdown to the next competition. The coach headline from §12.

**Tab 2 — Plan.** The week, phase-labelled, with the competition marked. Tap any day to see or edit it. The season calendar for Pro. A prominent **"Add a game"** button — this is the action that makes the app smart, so it should never be more than one tap away.

**Tab 3 — Improve.** The §8 skill menu, and the screen worth the most care. Sport (pre-filled) → a grid of that sport's named skills with plain labels and the rig illustrating each → "when is your next game?" → the dated block, each item explaining why it is there. Saved blocks live here too.

**Tab 4 — Library.** Browse every item in the downloaded packs, filterable by quality, equipment, surface and muscle. A body-map picker: tap a muscle to filter. Item detail shows the looping pose pair, the red muscle map, setup, execution, cues, mistakes, progressions and substitutes. Undownloaded sports appear here with a download button, gated by §4.

**Tab 5 — Me.** Sports and positions, season dates, trends (sleep first — §11), load chart, history, equipment, coach-supervision toggle, subscription, HealthKit, export for Pro, privacy policy, terms, and **delete account**.

**The live session screen** gets the most attention after Today: current item with its pose animation and muscle map, prefilled targets from last time, big steppers, an automatic rest timer, collapsible cues, next and previous. One-handed, sweaty-hands, glanceable, screen stays awake. It ends with the single RPE question and nothing else.

**Design rules.** Dynamic Type honoured everywhere. Full VoiceOver labels, especially on the muscle map, which is otherwise pure colour. Dark mode as the primary theme. Haptics on set completion and rest end. Plain language throughout — write for a 15-year-old in a locker room, not a strength coach with a clipboard.


## 16. Apple Watch

watchOS 10+. The Watch is the **primary logging surface during a session** — the phone is in a locker, a bag, or banned from the gym floor.

**It must work with no phone and no signal.** The Watch keeps the current session's plan and every logged set locally, and drains to the backend directly over Wi-Fi or LTE when it can, via the phone when it cannot. Every queued item carries the same `clientId` from §14, so retries cannot duplicate. An app that silently loses a logged set is worse than no Watch app.

**Keeping it alive.** Start an `HKWorkoutSession` with an `HKLiveWorkoutBuilder` when the session begins, with the workout type mapped from the sport (`.hockey`, `.soccer`, `.traditionalStrengthTraining` for gym work). That is what keeps the app foregrounded between sets, and it gives heart rate and active energy free. Ending it writes one `HKWorkout` — store its UUID on `Session.healthKitWorkoutId` so the phone never writes a duplicate.

**Screens**, a vertical paging `TabView`:

- **Current item** — name, target, Digital Crown to adjust, tap to log. Largest possible target.
- **Rest timer** — countdown ring, distinct haptic at zero, auto-starts on logging.
- **Session list** — what is left, tap to jump.
- **Heart rate and elapsed.**

**The morning check-in belongs on the wrist too.** Four taps on a watch face complication is a meaningfully lower barrier than opening the phone, and it is the single thing that most determines whether the daily loop survives past week two.

**Complication** (`WidgetKit`, corner, circular and rectangular): today's session, or the check-in prompt if it is not done, or a countdown to the next game. Reads from the shared App Group container — which means the App Group entitlement must be on the Watch app **and** the complication target, a thing that silently fails to ship if `project.yml` is wrong (§19).

**Payload compatibility rule.** Anything synced between phone and Watch must be **optional-with-default on both the JSON and plist decode paths**, with a CI test decoding an old payload with a new binary and vice versa. The two update at different times on real devices; a required field added on one side is a crash on the other.


## 17. HealthKit

**Setup.** HealthKit capability on the App ID, `com.apple.developer.healthkit` in the entitlements file. Both usage strings are mandatory or the app crashes on first access: `NSHealthShareUsageDescription` and `NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription`, each saying plainly what is read or written and why. Do **not** put `healthkit` in `UIRequiredDeviceCapabilities` — the app works fine without it, and declaring it blocks installs unnecessarily.

**Read:** sleep analysis (the important one), workouts, active energy, heart rate, resting heart rate, body mass (only if the athlete opts in), date of birth. **Write:** workouts, active energy.

**Sleep is why HealthKit is here at all.** If the athlete wears the Watch overnight, `HKCategoryTypeIdentifier.sleepAnalysis` gives real hours asleep, which turns the check-in's first question from a guess into a confirmation and makes the sleep-versus-load chart in §11 genuinely accurate. Handle all the stage values (`asleepCore`, `asleepDeep`, `asleepREM`, `awake`, `inBed`) and sum the asleep stages rather than trusting `inBed`.

**Apple's rules, which have teeth (5.1.3):**

- HealthKit data may **never** be used for advertising or marketing, or sold or shared with anyone. This app has no third parties at all, so this is satisfied by construction.
- It may **not** be stored in iCloud. Our own backend is permitted; be deliberate about what leaves the device — this plan syncs sleep _hours_ and workout summaries, never raw heart-rate series.
- The privacy policy must describe the HealthKit usage specifically.
- The app must work fully for someone who refuses every permission. Every call path needs a graceful denied branch — the check-in simply asks for sleep hours manually.

**Read authorisation status is deliberately unknowable.** Apple will not tell you whether a read was denied, to avoid leaking a condition. Design for "the query returned nothing", never for "permission denied". Request the narrowest set of types actually used; every extra type is another thing a 15-year-old's parent has to trust you with.


## 18. Subscriptions

**StoreKit 2, one subscription group, two products** — monthly and annual, annual at roughly 60% of twelve months. Price it for a teenager's pocket money or a parent's easy yes; §23 records the default assumption.

**Client.** `Product.products(for:)` to load, `product.purchase()` to buy, `Transaction.currentEntitlements` on launch, and a **`Transaction.updates`**** listener started at launch and never cancelled** — miss it and an Ask to Buy approval that lands while the app is closed never registers, which for this audience is the _common_ case, not an edge case. Call `transaction.finish()` only after the entitlement is recorded.

**The server is the authority.** `POST /billing/appstore/notify` receives App Store Server Notifications V2, verifies the JWS `signedPayload` against Apple's certificate chain, and is the **only** code path that writes `proUntil`. It handles renewals, cancellations, refunds, billing retry and grace period with the app closed. Reconcile on demand against the App Store Server API. Never trust a client-supplied receipt, and never keep entitlement only in `UserDefaults`.

**Grace period and billing retry keep Pro active**, with a non-blocking banner. Cutting a paying family off mid-retry turns a card expiry into a cancellation.

**Ask to Buy is the default path here, so design for it.** A purchase can sit pending for hours or days while a parent approves. The UI must show "waiting for approval" as a real state — not a failure, not a spinner, not a silent nothing — and unlock the moment the `Transaction.updates` listener fires. Getting this wrong is the single most likely subscription bug in an app for minors.

**Two things that must exist before submission.** A **demo account with ****`proUntil`**** set far in the future server-side**, with credentials in the review notes and a line saying no purchase is needed to see Pro features — reviewers use sandbox accounts, purchases there fail often, and an app whose paid features cannot be seen gets rejected under 2.1. And the paywall itself must be fully readable without purchasing, with price, period, and the Terms and Privacy links on that screen.

**Also required in App Store Connect:** Terms of Use (EULA) and Privacy Policy URLs on the app listing, and the same two links in-app on the paywall. Missing in-app links is the most common subscription rejection there is.


## 19. Build pipeline

Set this up in milestone 0, before any feature. Every item here is a mistake already paid for on a previous app.

**The constraint.** If building from a Linux box with no Mac, **GitHub Actions on ****`macos-26`**** is the only Swift compiler available.** Every iOS action is a `workflow_dispatch` workflow fired with `gh workflow run`, so make every run print evidence rather than pass/fail. Anything that is only HTTPS (App Store Connect calls) runs on `ubuntu-latest` in seconds instead of macOS minutes. `gh workflow run` 404s for a minute or two after a workflow file is first pushed — retry in a loop rather than concluding it is broken.

**Project generation.** XcodeGen from `project.yml`; never commit the `.xcodeproj`. Four targets: app, Watch app, Watch complication, and a widget extension. Two silent traps, both of which have shipped broken builds:

- An `entitlements:` block **does not** set `CODE_SIGN_ENTITLEMENTS`. Set it explicitly in each target's `settings.base`, or the file is generated and then never applied — HealthKit, App Groups and Sign in with Apple all silently absent from the signed app.
- An `entitlements:` block with only a `path` and no `properties` makes XcodeGen **overwrite that file with an empty 181-byte plist on every generate.** Always declare `properties`.
- watchOS targets need `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY: "4"`. Inheriting the project-wide `"1,2"` ships a watch binary claiming iPhone, which App Review rejects under 2.3 as "will not install on the device used in review".

**Capabilities must be on the App ID before the first archive**, because automatic signing builds the profile from the entitlements file and a missing capability fails the _build_. This app needs: **HealthKit, Sign in with Apple, In-App Purchase, App Groups, Push Notifications** (for the morning check-in reminder). Two specifics:

- **App Groups cannot be associated with an App ID by an API key.** Enabling the capability works; attaching the group is a manual portal step. Skipping it fails the archive with `Authentication failed … bearer token` on whichever target needed it.
- **Sign in with Apple is rejected as a bare capability** (409, "select at least one configuration"). It needs settings `[{key: APPLE_ID_AUTH_APP_CONSENT, options: [{key: PRIMARY_APP_CONSENT}]}]`.

**Signing in CI.** Import one Distribution `.p12` from a secret and let `-allowProvisioningUpdates` manage profiles. Do **not** let it mint a certificate per run: every run starts with an empty keychain, Xcode asks Apple for a new certificate, and the account hits its 15-certificate ceiling and builds stop dead. The `.p12` can be produced **with no Mac** — `openssl` CSR, upload, Apple issues the `.cer`, recombine. A keychain _export_ can never work unattended (`security export` blocks on a GUI dialog). On import pass `-T /usr/bin/codesign -T /usr/bin/security` **and** run `set-key-partition-list`; with only one, signing raises an unanswerable prompt. Never force `CODE_SIGN_IDENTITY` — Xcode refuses it as "conflicting provisioning settings".

**Workflows**

| Workflow | Runner | Does |
|---|---|---|
| `check.yml` | macos | XcodeGen, unsigned build of app and Watch, all §21 check executables, simulator screenshots as artefacts |
| `testflight.yml` | macos | Archive, export, `xcrun altool --upload-app`, then **print ****`codesign -d --entitlements :-`**** for the app and every nested bundle** plus Apple's `DistributionSummary.plist` |
| `packs.yml` | ubuntu | Regenerate the content packs from source data, verify checksums, publish |
| `asc-inspect.yml` | ubuntu | Read-only: App ID capabilities, versions, attached build, and what App Store Connect recorded inside a build |

**App Store Connect API in ten lines**, no SDK. The detail that matters: sign the ES256 JWT with `dsaEncoding: 'ieee-p1363'`. The default is DER, which every endpoint rejects as an invalid token without saying why. Also: `filter[identifier]` on `bundleIds` is a **prefix** match and `included` pools capabilities across matches, so narrow to the exact bundle's own relationship ids; and `GET /v1/builds/{id}/buildBundles` is forbidden for API keys while `GET /v1/builds/{id}?include=buildBundles` returns the same records.

**Backend deployment.** Docker Compose, with `prisma migrate deploy` in the image `CMD` so a redeploy can never run against a stale schema. Install async-rejection forwarding before any route — Express 4 ignores a rejected async handler, so the request hangs until the proxy 502s with nothing in the log.


## 20. Privacy, legal and App Review

A health app used by minors is the most scrutinised combination on the App Store. Build all of this before the first submission rather than collecting rejections.

**Must exist in the app**

- **Age gate on first launch** (§2), before any account is created.
- **Account deletion in-app**, never behind the paywall (5.1.1(v)), and it must actually delete — check-ins, sessions, logs, plans and the account row in one transaction. Relations do not cascade by default, so a naive delete fails on a foreign key the moment the athlete has logged one session.
- **Sign in with Apple** as the only login, which satisfies 4.8 outright.
- **The standing disclaimer** from §2, visible in onboarding, in Settings, and on every generated plan.
- **A real Support URL** — a page answering actual questions. A marketing home page has been rejected for this under 1.5.

**Privacy policy — and this app's is unusually easy to write honestly.** Cover: Sign in with Apple stores only an opaque identifier, never an email or name; wellness and training data is stored on our own servers and shared with nobody; HealthKit data is never used for advertising and never sold; there are no analytics, no trackers, no advertising identifiers and no third-party SDKs of any kind; and data is deleted permanently on request. Every one of those sentences is true by construction rather than by policy, which is the point of §3.

**Storing minors' health data — what that obligates.** Encryption at rest and in transit. A hard-delete path that actually deletes rather than soft-flagging. Retention limited to what renders history. No backups to anywhere outside our own database. And a written answer ready for the question "what happens to a 15-year-old's sleep data", because a reviewer may well ask.

**Age rating.** Answer the questionnaire honestly; this should land at 12+ or 17+ depending on how the health-information questions are answered. **Do not target the Kids Category** — it forbids exactly the analytics-free architecture's few remaining conveniences and imposes parental gates throughout; §23 records the decision to require 13+ instead.

**Privacy nutrition labels.** Declare health and fitness data, identifiers, and purchases. Linked to the user, **not** used for tracking. Getting this wrong is a metadata rejection and it is checked.

**Review notes template**, under 4000 characters, headed sections, naming the guideline each answers:

> DEMO ACCOUNT — credentials, plus "Pro is already enabled on this account; no purchase is needed." · WHAT THE APP IS · HOW TO REVIEW in five minutes (pick a sport, do the check-in, open today's session, use the Improve menu, add a game and watch the week change) · AGE (§2): 13+ gate, what is collected, what is never collected · HEALTH DATA (5.1.3): what is read, what is written, never used for advertising or shared · NO MEDICAL CLAIMS (1.4.1): the app never predicts injury, diagnoses, or advises return to play — state this explicitly, a reviewer looking at a teen health app will look for it · SUBSCRIPTIONS · SERVICES USED: none, there are no third parties · OTHER.

Count the characters in a file rather than estimating.

| What | How |
|---|---|

## 23. Decisions already made

Recorded so the build never stops to ask. Each is changeable later by editing the noted constant; none is structural.

- **Minimum age is 13.** Under-13 support would mean COPPA verifiable parental consent and the Kids Category — a materially different app. Out of scope (§2).
- **Sign in with Apple is the only login.** No email, no password, no other provider. Satisfies 4.8, collects the least possible, and the verification code is already written and proven.
- **There is a backend**, because accounts must survive a new phone and packs must update without an App Store release — but the client is offline-first and every daily action works with no signal (§3).
- **A free user always has one sport downloaded and fully usable offline.** Gating an athlete out of logging at a rink loses them permanently; §4 gates breadth and depth instead.
- **All 70+ sports ship.** Tiering affects skill-menu depth only, never whether a sport works.
- **No injury prediction, ever**, in any wording. Enforced by the §21 language lint, not just by intention.
- **No calorie deficits under 18, no body composition, no progress photos**, in any tier (§13).
- **Illustrations are procedural** — no artist, no licence, no budget, nothing to decide (§9).
- **No imported dataset of any kind.** Sports, drills, exercises and fuelling guidance are authored in-repo (§7).
- **Price:** assume roughly €5–7/month with annual at ~60% of twelve months — lower than an adult fitness app, because the buyer is a teenager or their parent. One constant; change it in App Store Connect without a build.
- **English only at launch.** The `locale` field exists for later, but no language switch appears until a full translation exists — nothing half-translated ships.
- **watchOS 10 minimum**, for device reach.
- **Backend runs on its own server and database**, separate from any other product, so one outage cannot touch the other.

**What this plan cannot do for you.** Everything above is buildable end to end with no external account, no licence and no commissioned art. Submission is different: it needs the Apple Developer account (you have one), the subscription products typed into App Store Connect, and a human to press Submit and answer whatever review says. That is not a missing feature — it is the one step that needs Apple's login rather than code.

**One genuine unknown, stated plainly.** Nobody can guarantee how App Review will treat a health-adjacent app used by minors; it is the most judgement-dependent area on the store. §2, §11 and §20 are written to remove every reason for a rejection I can anticipate, but budget for one round of back-and-forth rather than assuming first-time approval.
